Server to PDU power cord

By the RackMatch team · Updated 2026-11-10 · Sources linked on the page

The jumper is chosen from two facts: the inlet on the PSU, and the outlet set on the PDU. IEC C13/C14 and C19/C20 are the two pairs used in most racks.

PSU inlet C14Need a PDU C13 outlet and a C13–C14 jumper
PSU inlet C20Need a PDU C19 outlet and a C19–C20 jumper
C14 inlet + C19-only PDUC13–C20 jumper; still 10 A on the C13/C14 side
On this page
  1. Which end goes where

Which end goes where

C13 into the server C14 inlet. C14 into a PDU C13 outlet. C19 into a server C20 inlet. C20 into a PDU C19 outlet.

Worked pair: Dell PowerEdge R650 + APC AP8941. C13 into the R650. C14 into one C13 outlet. The C19 outlets on that PDU are not for this PSU.

Length

Vendor default jumpers are often 0.6 m, 2 m or about 2 m (HPE A0K02A is 6 ft). A Zero-U PDU in a shallow rack needs a much shorter cord so the loop does not fight the rails.

Related: What power cord does my server need, PDU power cord compatibility.

Read the PSU nameplate. The same chassis SKU can ship C14 or C20.

Frequently asked questions

What cord connects a server to a PDU?

A jumper: C13 to C14 for C14-inlet servers on C13 outlets, C19 to C20 for C20-inlet servers on C19 outlets, or C13 to C20 for C14-inlet servers on C19 outlets.

What length should rack jumpers be?

As short as the route allows: often 0.6–1.2 m for 0U PDUs at the back of the same rack, 1.8–2 m when the PDU is further away.

Can I use the wall cord that came with the server?

Only if the PDU has matching wall-type sockets. Rack PDUs with C13/C19 outlets need IEC jumpers.
