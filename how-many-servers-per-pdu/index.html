How many servers per PDU?

By the RackMatch team · Updated 2026-11-10 · Sources linked on the page

Quick answer: use the PDU calculator.

Two limits decide it: how much power the PDU can deliver, and how many outlets of the right type it has. The lower of the two wins.

1. Usable PDU power

Single-phase: volts × amps. In North America, keep continuous load at 80% of the breaker, so multiply by 0.8. Three-phase: volts × amps × 1.732 (× 0.8 in North America).

APC AP8941208 V, 30 A single-phase208 × 30 × 0.8 ≈ 5.0 kW
APC AP8853230 V, 32 A single-phase7.4 kW rated

2. Real server draw, not PSU rating

A server with two 1100 W PSUs does not draw 2200 W. Use the vendor's power calculator (Dell EIPT, HPE Power Advisor, Lenovo LCP) or a measured peak from the BMC. Add about 20% margin for spikes and future upgrades.

3. A/B redundancy

With dual PSUs on two PDUs (A feed and B feed), each PDU must be able to carry the whole rack if the other feed fails. So the total rack load must fit on one PDU, not two.

4. Outlets

Each server with two PSUs uses one outlet on PDU A and one on PDU B. A C14-inlet PSU needs a C13 outlet. A C20-inlet PSU needs a C19 outlet, and PDUs usually have only 3–6 of those.

Worked example

A/B pair of APC AP8941 (5.0 kW usable each, 21 C13 + 3 C19). Servers measured at 450 W peak, plus 20% margin = 540 W. Power limit: 5000 / 540 ≈ 9 servers. Outlet limit: 21. Answer: 9 servers per A/B pair. Power runs out long before outlets do.

GPU servers flip this: one 8-GPU system can need 6 × C19 outlets and more than 10 kW, so a single PDU may not be enough. See DGX H100 PDU requirements.

Outlet banks and breakers

Most rack PDUs split their outlets into banks, and each bank has its own breaker, often 16 A or 20 A. A PDU can have plenty of total capacity and still trip one bank if you load all the heavy servers on the same group of outlets. Spread high-draw servers across banks and read the per-bank current on metered PDUs.

Three-phase PDUs: balance the phases

On a three-phase PDU the outlets are wired to different phases (or phase pairs at 208 V). Full capacity is only available when the three phases carry roughly the same load. Put the same number of similar servers on each phase group, and check per-phase current after the rack is live.

Mistakes that make the count wrong

  1. Adding up PSU nameplate watts. It overstates the load and wastes PDU capacity.
  2. Splitting the load across A and B and calling it double capacity. In a failure one side takes everything.
  3. Forgetting C19 outlets. GPU and high-watt servers can run out of C19s before they run out of power.
  4. Powering the whole rack on at once. Inrush current on start-up can trip a breaker; stagger power-on where the BMC allows it.
  5. Ignoring growth. A rack filled to 100% on day one has no room for the next server.
Rules on derating differ by country and code. Ask the site electrician for the branch circuit limits.

Frequently asked questions

How many servers can one PDU power?

Divide the usable PDU power by the real server draw. Usable power is volts × amps (× 1.732 for three-phase), reduced to 80% for continuous load in North America.

Should I use nameplate watts or measured watts?

Measured or vendor-calculated typical draw gives a realistic count. Nameplate watts give a safe worst case. Use measured draw plus a margin of 20–30%.

Does A/B redundancy change the count?

Yes. With two PDUs each one must carry the whole load alone if the other feed fails, so size each PDU for the full rack.
