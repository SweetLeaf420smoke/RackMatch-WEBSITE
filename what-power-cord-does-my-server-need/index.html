What power cord does my server need?

By the RackMatch team · Updated 2026-11-10 · Sources linked on the page

Do not start from the chassis model alone. Start from the PSU that is installed.

  1. Read the inlet on the PSU: C14 or C20 (or a locking variant of those).
  2. Read volts and amps on the same nameplate.
  3. Look at the PDU outlet: C13, C19, or both.
  4. Pick the jumper that joins those two faces.

On a C14 inlet, the C13 coupler goes into the server and the C14 plug goes into a C13 outlet on the PDU. On a C20 inlet, the C19 coupler goes into the server and the C20 plug goes into a C19 outlet. See C13 vs C14 and C19 vs C20.

Examples on this site: Dell PowerEdge R650 is C14 / C13–C14 in the common SKU. Dell PowerEdge R760 is C13–C14 on typical 10 A jumpers Dell sells for the chassis, and C19–C20 on the 2400 W PSU. DGX H100 is locking C19/C20, 250 VAC, 20 A, 1.2 m, six cords in the ship kit. One server plus one PDU: R650 + AP8941.

Use the matcher on the home page when you have both a server model and a PDU model from the catalog.

Where to read the inlet

  • The PSU label: look for the input rating. About 10 A on a C14 inlet, up to 16 A on a C20 inlet.
  • The back of the PSU: C14 has bevelled corners and upright pins; C20 is rectangular with flat pins (see the drawing above).
  • The vendor spec sheet or owner's manual, in the power supply table. Many list the inlet per PSU wattage.
  • The order or service tag record, which lists the PSU option that shipped.

Cord length for rack jumpers

LayoutTypical length
0U vertical PDU at the back of the same rack, server close to the outlet0.6–1.0 m
0U PDU, outlet far from the server or cable arm in use1.2–1.5 m
Horizontal 1U PDU or PDU in the next rack1.8–2.0 m

Short cords keep airflow clear and make A/B cabling easy to trace. Colour-coded cords (one colour per feed) help even more.

Plugging into a wall socket instead of a PDU

Then the cord has a wall plug on one end and C13 or C19 on the other. Pick the plug for your country:

  • USA and Canada: NEMA 5-15P to C13. A C19 PSU on 120 V usually needs a 20 A circuit and a NEMA 5-20P cord.
  • Most of Europe: CEE 7/7 (Schuko) to C13 or C19.
  • UK and Ireland: BS 1363 to C13. BS 1363 plugs are fused at 13 A or less, which limits a C19 PSU.
  • South Africa: SANS 164-1 (Type M) or SANS 164-2 (Type N) to C13.

Checklist before you order

  1. Inlet type on every installed PSU.
  2. Outlet type on both PDUs, and how many free C13 and C19 outlets each has.
  3. Number of PSUs per server, so A and B cords add up.
  4. Length for each rack position.
  5. Locking requirement, if the site or PDU uses locking outlets.
If the nameplate and the vendor PDF disagree with a web SKU list, trust the nameplate.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find out which power cord my server needs?

Read the inlet on the installed PSU (C14 or C20), then the outlet type on the PDU (C13 or C19), and pick the jumper that joins them.

Does the server model decide the cord?

No. The same chassis can ship with PSUs that have different inlets depending on wattage.

What if I plug the server into a wall socket?

Then you need a cord with the wall plug for your country on one end and C13 or C19 on the other.
